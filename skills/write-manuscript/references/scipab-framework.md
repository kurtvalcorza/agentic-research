# SCIPAB Framework: Strategic Storytelling for Presentations

## Overview

**SCIPAB** is a persuasive presentation structure that replaces the traditional "Intro → Problem → Solution → Conclusion" format with a narrative that makes audiences **feel the stakes** before presenting your position.

**Origin:** Adapted from strategic consulting frameworks (McKinsey, BCG) and narrative theory.

**Core insight:** Audiences don't act on logical arguments alone—they need to viscerally understand **what happens if we don't act** (Implication) before they'll commit to your solution.

---

## The Six Elements

### 1. Situation (Where are we?)

**Purpose:** Establish shared reality. Neutral, factual, uncontroversial.

**Questions to answer:**
- What's the current state?
- What context does the audience need?
- What's normal/expected right now?

**Tone:** Objective, calm, descriptive.

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Situation: Researchers across the consortium store and manage datasets
independently—genomics, climate, survey data. Current practice is for
each university to maintain its own data store, on a per-institution basis.
```

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Starting with the problem (creates defensiveness)
- ❌ Assuming audience knows the context (they don't)
- ❌ Being too brief (audience needs grounding)

**How many slides:** 1-3 slides (depending on complexity)

---

### 2. Complication (What's broken?)

**Purpose:** Introduce tension. Show what's wrong, inefficient, or at risk.

**Questions to answer:**
- What changed? (What broke the Situation?)
- What's the specific problem?
- Why is the status quo unsustainable?

**Tone:** Concerned, urgent (but not alarmist).

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Complication: 8 universities are maintaining separate data silos.
- Total cost: $1.2M/year in storage and re-collection
- Zero reuse (teams re-collect datasets peers already hold)
- No shared infrastructure (incompatible formats and access rules)
- Reproducibility risk (datasets unversioned and hard to cite)
```

**Visual cues:**
- Dark or red color scheme (tension)
- Show fragmentation (e.g., pie chart of 8 silos)
- Use words like "scattered," "duplicated," "vulnerable"

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Presenting multiple unrelated problems (pick ONE complication)
- ❌ Being too abstract ("inefficiency" → what kind?)
- ❌ Jumping to solutions here (save for Position)

**How many slides:** 1-2 slides (the "pain" slide)

---

### 3. Implication (What happens if we don't act?)

**Purpose:** Make the audience **feel** the stakes. This is the heart of SCIPAB.

**Questions to answer:**
- So what? Why does the Complication matter?
- What's the **specific** consequence of inaction?
- What does the future look like if we do nothing?

**Tone:** Urgent, visceral (paint the dystopia).

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Implication: If we continue this trajectory for 5 years:
- $6M cumulative cost of duplicated data work (budget hemorrhage)
- Researchers keep re-collecting the same datasets (wasted effort compounds)
- No shared standards or governance emerge (fragmentation locks in)
- Reproducibility gaps widen (results built on un-citable, unversioned data)

→ the consortium stays a collection of isolated silos, never a shared research platform.
```

**Why this works:**
- "$6M" is concrete (not "we'll fall behind")
- "Re-collecting the same datasets" is tangible (visible waste)
- "Isolated silos forever" is identity-threatening (collaboration breaks down)

**Visual cues:**
- Bold, large text
- Timeline showing worsening trajectory
- Use red/dark theme
- Contrast "now" vs. "5 years from now"

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Generic stakes ("we'll fall behind" → behind whom, in what?)
- ❌ Logical consequences only (no emotional resonance)
- ❌ Too many implications (pick the ONE your audience cares about)

**How many slides:** 1 slide (high impact, brief)

---

### 4. Position (What should we believe?)

**Purpose:** Present your thesis. The "solution" to the Complication.

**Questions to answer:**
- What's your core argument?
- What should the audience believe is true?
- Why is your approach the right one?

**Tone:** Confident, clear, evidence-backed.

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Position: A federated open-data platform provides shared, reusable
research data infrastructure for the university consortium.

Core claims:
1. Cost reduction: 67% cheaper than duplicated storage ($0.4M vs. 1.2M/year)
2. Reuse and collaboration: 8 universities sharing one federated catalog
3. Reproducibility: Datasets versioned and citable across institutions
4. Interoperability: One metadata standard across all member repositories
```

**Structure:**
- Lead with the thesis (1 sentence)
- Support with 3-5 key claims
- Each claim backed by evidence (data, case studies, expert testimony)

**Visual cues:**
- Neutral or branded colors (consortium blue/green)
- Use "framework" visuals (e.g., pillars, layers, cycles)
- Show comparisons (before/after, current/proposed)

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Weak thesis ("We should explore AI" → What specifically?)
- ❌ Too many claims (audience can't remember >5)
- ❌ No evidence (assertions without proof)

**How many slides:** 3-5 slides (the "meat" of the deck)

---

### 5. Action (What's the first step?)

**Purpose:** Make it concrete. What does the audience do **next**?

**Questions to answer:**
- What's the immediate, achievable action?
- Who does it? By when?
- What's required to start? (budget, approval, resources)

**Tone:** Directive, specific, achievable.

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Action: Launch a 6-month pilot with 3 member universities.

Details:
- Timeline: Q2 2026 (April-September)
- Budget: $0.5M (onboarding + infrastructure)
- Scope: Federate 3 shared datasets (genomics, climate, survey)
- Success criteria: 50% cost reduction, 3 universities onboarded
- Decision: Consortium steering committee approval required by March 2026
```

**Why "6-month pilot" works:**
- It's reversible (low risk)
- It's time-bound (urgency)
- It's scoped (not "federate everything everywhere")
- It has clear metrics (success criteria)

**Visual cues:**
- Timeline graphic (Gantt chart, roadmap)
- Bullet points with owners ("Who: platform engineering lead")
- Use action verbs ("Launch," "Federate," "Onboard")

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Vague actions ("We should adopt the platform" → How? When? Who?)
- ❌ Too ambitious ("Federate all 8 universities at once" → Unrealistic first step)
- ❌ No owners ("Someone should..." → Who specifically?)

**How many slides:** 1 slide (clear CTA)

---

### 6. Benefit (What do we gain?)

**Purpose:** Paint the utopia. Show the positive future if we act.

**Questions to answer:**
- What's the end state?
- What does success look like?
- How is this future better than today?

**Tone:** Optimistic, inspiring, concrete.

**Example (open-data platform deck):**
```
Benefit: By 2028, the consortium has a shared open-science repository.

Outcomes:
- $0.8M/year savings (reallocated to research, not duplicated storage)
- 200 datasets shared and citable across member institutions
- 8 universities using the platform (standardized, interoperable data infra)
- Sector leadership: the consortium becomes a reference model for open-science data sharing

→ Research community as collaborator and sharer, not isolated silos.
```

**Why this works:**
- Quantified benefits ($0.8M, 200 datasets, 8 universities)
- Identity shift ("collaborator, not silo")
- Sector leadership angle (reference model)

**Visual cues:**
- Bright, uplifting colors (green, blue gradients)
- "After" visuals (contrast with Complication's "before")
- Use aspirational language ("leadership," "capability," "reproducibility")

**Common mistakes:**
- ❌ Repeating the Position (Benefit is the outcome, not the solution)
- ❌ Vague benefits ("Things will be better" → How?)
- ❌ Unrealistic promises (overpromising kills credibility)

**How many slides:** 1 slide (the "inspiration" slide)

## SCIPAB vs. Traditional Structure

| Traditional | SCIPAB | Why SCIPAB Wins |
|-------------|--------|----------------|
| Intro | Situation | Grounds audience in shared reality |
| Problem | Complication | Shows what's broken (tension) |
| *(missing)* | **Implication** | **Makes audience feel the stakes** |
| Solution | Position | Presents thesis (backed by evidence) |
| Next Steps | Action | Concrete, achievable first step |
| Conclusion | Benefit | Inspiring future state |

**Key difference:** The **Implication** forces the audience to confront "what happens if we don't act?" before you present your solution. This creates **urgency** and **receptivity**.

---

## When to Use SCIPAB

### Perfect for:
- ✅ **Persuasive presentations** (budget approvals, policy proposals)
- ✅ **Executive briefings** (leadership needs clear stakes)
- ✅ **Project proposals** (why this project, why now?)
- ✅ **Research with policy implications** (academic → action)

### Not ideal for:
- ❌ **Status updates** (no persuasion needed)
- ❌ **Educational content** (teaching concepts, not advocating)
- ❌ **Technical deep-dives** (focus is "how," not "why")

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. **Weak Implication**
**Bad:** "If we don't act, we'll fall behind in research."
- Too vague ("fall behind" whom? in what?)
- No emotional resonance

**Good:** "In 5 years, teams will have spent $6M re-collecting datasets peers already hold, and results will keep failing to reproduce because the underlying data is un-citable."
- Specific (concrete waste, reproducibility failure)
- Tangible (visible, repeated effort)

---

### 2. **Too Many Complications**
**Bad:** Listing 7 different problems (budget, duplication, security, compliance, format chaos, reproducibility, collaboration)
- Audience is overwhelmed
- Message is diluted

**Good:** Pick ONE complication (e.g., duplication cost) and focus there.
- If you must mention others, nest them as sub-points under the main complication

---

### 3. **Vague Action**
**Bad:** "We should adopt the open-data platform."
- Not actionable (How? When? Who?)
- Outcome, not action

**Good:** "Launch a 6-month pilot with 3 member universities by Q2 2026 (steering committee approval needed by March)."
- Specific, time-bound, clear owner

---

### 4. **Position = Feature List**
**Bad:** "The platform has 15 features: federated catalog, ingest pipeline, access control, monitoring dashboard, ..."
- Feature dump (not strategic)
- Audience can't remember

**Good:** "The platform provides 3 things: cost reduction (67%), reuse across institutions, and reproducibility."
- Strategic claims (not features)
- Memorable (rule of 3)

## Adapting SCIPAB by Audience

### Academic Audience
- **Situation:** Longer (2-3 slides) to establish research context
- **Complication:** Focus on knowledge gaps or methodological limitations
- **Implication:** Impact on the field ("If unsolved, this blocks progress on X")
- **Position:** Heavily cited (evidence-based claims)
- **Action:** Future research directions (not policy)
- **Benefit:** Contribution to the field

### Executive Audience
- **Situation:** Brief (1 slide) — they know the context
- **Complication:** Focus on budget, risk, or competitive pressure
- **Implication:** Business/financial stakes ("We lose $X" or "Competitor gains Y")
- **Position:** High-level claims (3-5 bullets, no technical depth)
- **Action:** Clear decision point ("Approve budget by [date]")
- **Benefit:** ROI, market position, or strategic advantage

### Technical Audience
- **Situation:** Technical baseline (what's the current architecture?)
- **Complication:** Technical debt, scalability limits, or inefficiency
- **Implication:** System failure risk or unsustainable operations
- **Position:** Technical approach (architecture, algorithms, protocols)
- **Action:** Implementation plan (milestones, dependencies)
- **Benefit:** Improved performance, maintainability, or extensibility

## SCIPAB in Different Contexts

### Example 1: Research Paper Presentation

**Situation:** Current AI models for low-resource languages rely on transfer learning from high-resource languages.

**Complication:** Transfer learning fails for morphologically rich languages — accuracy drops by 30%.

**Implication:** Hundreds of millions of speakers of these languages are excluded from AI advances (digital divide widens).

**Position:** We propose a multilingual pre-training method that shares morphological features across related languages.

**Action:** Release pre-trained models and training code by Q2 2026 (enable replication).

**Benefit:** Close the accuracy gap (from 30% to 5%) and enable AI research in 50+ low-resource languages.

---

### Example 2: Budget Approval Presentation

**Situation:** Our QA process relies on manual testing (20 engineers, 2-week regression cycles).

**Complication:** As codebase grows (now 500K LOC), regression time increases linearly. We're at 3-week cycles.

**Implication:** In 12 months, regression will take 6 weeks (blocks all releases). Either we stop releasing or we ship broken code.

**Position:** Invest $800K in test automation infrastructure (CI/CD, Selenium grid, test data management).

**Action:** Approve Q1 budget ($800K) and hire 2 automation engineers by Feb 2026.

**Benefit:** Reduce regression to 2 hours (from 3 weeks), enable weekly releases, save $1.2M/year in QA labor.

---

### Example 3: Policy Proposal Presentation

**Situation:** The jurisdiction has no AI regulation framework (unlike peer regions that have enacted dedicated AI laws).

**Complication:** Without regulation, harmful AI systems are deployed (facial recognition without consent, biased hiring algorithms).

**Implication:** In 5 years, the jurisdiction faces:
- Privacy violations (no recourse for citizens)
- Discriminatory AI (hiring, lending, law enforcement)
- Reputational damage (seen as a "wild west" for AI)

**Position:** Establish an AI Safety Board (modeled on leading AI-governance frameworks) to oversee high-risk AI systems.

**Action:** Draft enabling legislation by Q2 2026 (for executive approval).

**Benefit:** The jurisdiction becomes a regional leader in AI governance, attracts responsible AI investment, and protects citizens from AI harms.

## Write Manuscript Slide Deck's Use of SCIPAB

**Phase 1 (Synthesis):**
- Extract SCIPAB elements from source materials
- Document in `STAGING.md` as "SCIPAB Seed"

**Phase 2 (Interrogation):**
- Enhance Writing mode challenges weak Implications and Actions
- Forces user to clarify stakes and concrete steps

**Phase 3 (Drafting):**
- Maps SCIPAB elements to slides
- Applies visual cues (red for Complication, bold for Implication, bright for Benefit)

**Result:** Strategically-framed deck, not generic slide dump.
