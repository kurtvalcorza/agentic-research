# Oxford CEBM Levels of Evidence

Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine (Oxford) evidence hierarchy for clinical and research contexts.

---

## Evidence Levels (2011 Version)

### Level 1 (Highest)
**1a:** Systematic reviews of RCTs with homogeneity
**1b:** Individual RCT with narrow confidence interval
**1c:** All-or-none case series

### Level 2
**2a:** Systematic reviews of cohort studies with homogeneity
**2b:** Individual cohort study or low-quality RCT
**2c:** Outcomes research; ecological studies

### Level 3
**3a:** Systematic reviews of case-control studies with homogeneity
**3b:** Individual case-control study

### Level 4
Case series, poor quality cohort, poor quality case-control

### Level 5 (Lowest)
Expert opinion without explicit critical appraisal

## Study Design Detection

### RCT Indicators
- "Randomized" or "randomly assigned"
- Control group with intervention
- Prospective design
- Blinding mentioned

### Cohort Indicators
- "Followed" or "follow-up"
- Exposure measured before outcome
- No intervention by researchers
- Prospective or retrospective

### Case-Control Indicators
- "Cases" and "controls"
- Outcome already occurred
- Looking backward for exposure
- Retrospective design

### Cross-Sectional Indicators
- "Survey" or "prevalence"
- Single time point
- Exposure and outcome measured together

## Applying Levels to Claims

### Level 1 Claims (Strongest)
- Supported by systematic reviews or high-quality RCTs
- Can use definitive language: "X causes Y"
- Example: "Systematic reviews confirm that..."

### Level 2 Claims
- Supported by cohort studies
- Use qualified language: "X is associated with Y"
- Example: "Cohort studies suggest..."

### Level 3-4 Claims
- Supported by case-control or case series
- Use cautious language: "X may be associated with Y"
- Example: "Case studies indicate possible..."

### Level 5 Claims
- Expert opinion or theoretical
- Use hedging: "X is theorized to..."
- Example: "Experts suggest..."

## Comparison: Oxford vs GRADE

| Oxford Level | Approximate GRADE |
|--------------|-------------------|
| 1a-1b | HIGH |
| 2a-2b | MODERATE |
| 3a-3b | LOW |
| 4-5 | VERY LOW |

**Note:** GRADE also considers factors beyond study design (bias, precision, etc.)

## Domain Applications

### Clinical Practice
- Level 1-2: Strong recommendations
- Level 3-4: Weak recommendations
- Level 5: Practice points only

### Policy Development
- Level 1-2: Evidence-based policy
- Level 3-4: Informed policy
- Level 5: Expert-informed only

### Research Priority
- Identify gaps where only Level 4-5 exists
- Prioritize topics needing Level 1-2 evidence

## Example Application

```markdown
## Evidence Assessment: AI in Healthcare

**Claim:** AI diagnostic tools improve accuracy

**Evidence Found:**
- 2 systematic reviews (Level 1a)
- 5 RCTs (Level 1b)
- 8 cohort studies (Level 2b)
- 15 case series (Level 4)

**Strongest Evidence:** Level 1a (SR of RCTs)

**Recommendation:** Strong evidence supports the claim.
Can use definitive language: "AI diagnostic tools
have been shown to improve accuracy."
```

---

## Limitations

1. **Hierarchy assumptions:** Not all RCTs are better than all observational studies
2. **Context dependency:** Some questions can't be answered by RCTs
3. **Quality variation:** Low-quality RCT may be worse than high-quality cohort
4. **Implementation:** Efficacy (RCT) vs effectiveness (real-world)

**Best practice:** Use Oxford levels as starting point, then apply GRADE-style quality assessment.
