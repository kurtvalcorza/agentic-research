# Provocation Bank for Contribution Framing

Tools for Thought provocations to challenge and strengthen contribution framing.

---

## Contribution Clarity Provocations

### The "So What" Test
> "You claim [contribution X]. So what? Why should anyone care?"

**Purpose:** Forces articulation of significance beyond the immediate finding.

### The Deletion Test
> "If this paper didn't exist, what knowledge would be lost? Be specific."

**Purpose:** Identifies whether contribution is genuinely novel or incremental.

### The Alternative Explanation
> "Could [alternative explanation] account for your findings equally well?"

**Purpose:** Tests robustness of theoretical claims.

### The Skeptic's Question
> "A senior scholar in [rival school] reads your paper. What's their first objection?"

**Purpose:** Anticipates and pre-empts criticism.

### The Context Boundary
> "In what contexts does your contribution NOT apply? Be honest."

**Purpose:** Prevents overclaiming and improves precision.

## Gap Validation Provocations

### The Real Gap Test
> "You identify [gap X]. Has anyone actually tried and failed to address this, or is it just understudied?"

**Purpose:** Distinguishes between genuine gaps and irrelevant gaps.

### The Importance Challenge
> "Why hasn't anyone studied this before? Lack of interest, or lack of capability?"

**Purpose:** Tests whether gap matters or was ignored for good reason.

### The Evidence Demand
> "You claim [gap exists]. Show me the systematic review or search strategy that confirms no prior work exists."

**Purpose:** Prevents false gap claims based on incomplete literature search.

### The Stakeholder Test
> "Who is harmed by this gap? Name specific groups affected."

**Purpose:** Grounds gap in real-world significance.

## Positioning Provocations

### The Spectrum Question
> "On a spectrum from 'confirming existing views' to 'revolutionizing the field,' where does your contribution honestly sit?"

**Purpose:** Calibrates claims to appropriate level.

### The Inheritance Test
> "Whose work are you building on? Give them credit. Now, what's distinctly YOURS?"

**Purpose:** Clarifies originality while acknowledging foundations.

### The 10-Year Test
> "In 10 years, will this paper still be cited? For what?"

**Purpose:** Identifies lasting vs. ephemeral contributions.

### The Practitioner Question
> "If a practitioner reads only your contribution statement, what would they do differently tomorrow?"

**Purpose:** Tests actionability for applied work.

---

## Limitation Provocations

### The Honest Weakness
> "What's the biggest weakness of this study that you're hoping reviewers won't notice?"

**Purpose:** Surfaces hidden limitations for proactive disclosure.

### The Replication Challenge
> "If someone tried to replicate this in a different context, what would likely fail?"

**Purpose:** Identifies context-dependent assumptions.

### The Alternative Method
> "If you had unlimited resources, what method would you have used instead? Why didn't you?"

**Purpose:** Acknowledges methodological trade-offs.

### The Missing Data
> "What data would have made your argument stronger but was unavailable?"

**Purpose:** Identifies evidence gaps.

---

## Implication Provocations

### The Cascade Question
> "If your finding is correct, what else must be true?"

**Purpose:** Explores logical implications.

### The Contradiction Test
> "What existing beliefs would have to be wrong for your contribution to be right?"

**Purpose:** Identifies theoretical tensions.

### The Policy Question
> "If a policymaker believed your findings, what should they change?"

**Purpose:** Translates academic contribution to real-world action.

### The Future Research Test
> "What's the ONE most important study that should follow this work?"

**Purpose:** Identifies logical next steps.

---

## Anti-Overclaiming Provocations

### The Language Audit
> "Replace every 'shows' with 'suggests' and every 'proves' with 'provides evidence for.' Does your paper still make sense?"

**Purpose:** Catches overclaiming language.

### The Single Study Warning
> "This is one study. What would it take to make the claims you're making?"

**Purpose:** Calibrates claims to evidence strength.

### The Counter-Example Request
> "Give me one plausible scenario where your findings wouldn't hold."

**Purpose:** Acknowledges boundary conditions.

### The Humility Check
> "Imagine you're reviewing this paper. What would make you recommend rejection?"

**Purpose:** Surfaces weaknesses through role reversal.

## Using Provocations Effectively

### When to Use
- After first draft of contribution statement
- When contribution feels vague or generic
- When claims seem either too bold or too weak
- Before finalizing discussion section

### How to Respond
1. Take the provocation seriously (don't dismiss)
2. Articulate your answer explicitly
3. Revise the contribution statement if needed
4. Document the reasoning for future reference

### Signs of Strong Response
- Specific rather than general
- Evidence-based rather than assertion
- Acknowledges limitations
- Connects to broader significance

### Signs of Weak Response
- Defensive or dismissive
- Generic platitudes
- Unable to articulate clearly
- Avoids the hard question
